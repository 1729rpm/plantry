# Reviewer report: Menu A (v6 proposed 10-week plan) vs Menu B (actual 8 cooked weeks)

Clean-room comparison of two menu files as data. Inputs were only `features/engine-v6-dry-run-menu.md` (Menu A) and `features/as-eaten-8-weeks.md` (Menu B). Every count below was tallied from the raw menu text and re-verified before being written down.

## Tabulation and normalization

Menu A: 10 weeks, 60 days, no skips. 50 breakfasts (Saturdays carry none), 60 lunches, 60 fruit entries. Weeks 1 to 5 are labeled Monsoon, weeks 6 to 10 Winter.

Menu B: 8 weeks, 44 days present of 48 possible (skipped: week 1 Friday, week 7 Monday and Tuesday, week 8 Monday). 36 breakfasts (Saturdays carry none), 44 lunches, 44 fruit entries. Six dishes are marked (custom).

All rates are normalized to occasions actually present: per lunch means divided by 60 for A and 44 for B, per breakfast by 50 and 36, per weekday lunch by 50 and 36, and per week by 10 and 8. Category counts are name-based (a dish counts as paneer if its name says paneer, and so on); where a name is ambiguous the treatment is stated. A's structural labels ((carb), (protein floor), (exploration), (special), (dessert), (favorite)) were used only to describe A's own structure, never to classify dishes.

## Findings, most systematic first

1. Paneer at lunch roughly quadruples, and the surge concentrates in A's back half. Menu A serves a paneer-named lunch dish 15 times in 60 lunches (0.25 per lunch, 1.5 per week); Menu B served 3 in 44 (0.068 per lunch, 0.38 per week). Counting every meal, A has 24 paneer rows in 10 weeks against B's 10 in 8 (2.4 vs 1.25 per week), and the entire gap is at lunch, since breakfast paneer rates match (finding restated in the same-in-both list). By week, A's paneer lunch counts run 1, 1, 0, 0, 2, 2, 3, 2, 3, 1; weeks 6 to 9 each carry 2 or 3 paneer lunches, and week 9 serves paneer four days running (Paneer lababdar Monday, Kadai paneer Tuesday, Paneer bhurji at Wednesday breakfast, Matar paneer Thursday). Five of A's 20 exploration picks are paneer dishes, which is where much of the surge enters. (Malai kofta was not counted as paneer under the name rule; it appears once in A and twice in B, so no counting choice changes the direction.)

2. International lunches drop to about half the household's actual rate, and Tuesday stops being international day. Counting non-Indian named lunch dishes including Indo-Chinese (pastas, Thai dishes, Singapore noodles, hakka noodles, fried rice, Manchurian, risotto, shakshuka, Spanish omelette, Vietnamese noodle salad; hummus excluded and handled separately), Menu B has 12 in 44 lunches (0.27 per lunch, 1.5 per week) and Menu A has 8 in 60 (0.13 per lunch, 0.8 per week). Placement changes as much as volume: B put an international plate on 5 of its 7 present Tuesdays (white sauce pasta, continental baked vegetables, Singapore noodles, veg hakka noodles, veg fried rice with Paneer Manchurian); A puts one on 2 of 10 Tuesdays. B also habitually paired an international carb main with a grilled protein (chicken tikka with white sauce pasta, grilled chicken with continental baked vegetables, with red sauce pasta, and with Singapore noodles, fish tikka with Singapore noodles, Paneer Manchurian with veg fried rice; 6 such plates); A produces that pairing twice (mushroom risotto with chicken breast, Vietnamese noodle salad with chicken tikka).

3. Egg mains migrate from breakfast to lunch. Menu A has 8 egg lunch dishes in 60 lunches (Egg pulao, Egg curry, Spanish omelette, Shakshuka, Egg roast, Egg masala dry, Egg bhurji keto, Egg salad; 0.8 per week); Menu B had exactly 1 in 44 (Egg biryani, one Saturday). Meanwhile eggs at breakfast fall from 17 of 36 breakfasts in B (47 percent) to 18 of 50 in A (36 percent), so total egg volume is close (2.6 vs 2.25 rows per week) but redistributed. The role of boiled eggs also flips: in B they are usually a rider on a grain main (first item on the plate 4 times, later item 8 times, including all four Vegetable sevai days); in A they are usually the plate's lead with only a chutney beside them (first item 7 times, later item 5).

4. Salads multiply and a new dish type appears, the protein salad. Menu A places a salad on the lunch plate 20 times in 60 lunches (0.33 per lunch, 2.0 per week): 14 vegetable salads plus 6 protein-bearing salads (Chicken salad, Prawn salad, Chicken breast salad, Paneer salad, Egg salad, Vietnamese noodle salad), five of them entering as the protein floor. Menu B had 6 salads in 44 lunches (0.14 per lunch, 0.75 per week), all plain vegetable salads; zero protein salads ever. Raita, by contrast, is level in both (see the same list), so this is specifically a salad inflation, 2.4x per occasion.

5. Lunch plates get bigger, driven by the appended protein floor. Menu A runs 4-item lunches on 16 of 60 days (27 percent) against B's 3 of 44 (7 percent), and B's three 4-item plates all fell in its first three weeks, then never recurred. Light 2-item weekday lunches shrink from 12 of 36 in B (33 percent) to 8 of 50 in A (16 percent). Average items per weekday lunch: A 2.96, B 2.58; per Saturday lunch: A 3.60, B 2.88. A's own labels show the mechanism: 23 protein-floor dishes are appended across the 10 weeks, a construct with no counterpart label in B. One knock-on effect: 37 of A's 50 weekday lunches carry a named animal-protein dish (74 percent) against 21 of B's 36 (58 percent).

6. Saturday hardens from a loose treat day into a fixed four-part template. Every A Saturday is special main plus companion plus dessert, with a protein dish appended on 6 of 10. Dessert: A 10 of 10, B 6 of 8 (B skipped dessert on the khichdi-with-mutton and fish-tikka-with-fried-rice Saturdays). A standing companion (raita 5, hummus 2, salad 3): A 10 of 10; B had a raita or salad on only 4 of 8 Saturdays, otherwise a second main or a sabzi. Four-item Saturdays: A 6 of 10, B 0 of 8. B's Saturday protein, when present (4 of 8), was the main itself (egg biryani, mutton pepper fry, fish tikka, grilled chicken); A's is an added side on an otherwise vegetarian special.

7. Fruit variety collapses in A's Winter half and loses B's headline fruit. A's Monsoon weeks are perfectly varied (6 distinct fruits each week, no repeats); its 5 Winter weeks then run exactly 3 fruits (banana, papaya, pomegranate) at exactly 10 servings each over 30 days, every week showing 3 distinct fruits twice each. B never went below 4 distinct fruits in any week (range 4 to 6 across its weeks, with 9 distinct fruits over 44 days). Concentration: A's top three fruits cover 42 of 60 days (70 percent); B's top three covered 24 of 44 (55 percent). Mango, B's most-eaten fruit at 11 of 44 days (25 percent), falls to 5 of 60 in A (8 percent, all in September); plausibly seasonal, but it is the single largest dish-level gap in the data.

8. A erases the household's day-of-week signatures and substitutes its own scheduling regularities. B's habits, each gone in A: plain roti at lunch on every present Friday (7 of 7; A spreads roti almost perfectly flat, 5 or 6 per weekday); seafood on Thursday (5 of 8 B Thursdays, prawn pepper fry 3 of 3 times and fish tikka twice, vs 2 of 10 A Thursdays); Vegetable sevai always Thursday and always with boiled eggs (4 of 4 in B; A serves sevai 4 times on 4 different weekdays); avocado toast on Tuesday (4 of its 6 B appearances; A spreads it over Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday). What A adds instead: avocado toast exactly once every week (10 of 10, labeled favorite; B managed 6 in 8 weeks); the two weekly exploration picks land on Monday or Tuesday 13 of 20 times (7 of 10 Mondays carry one, making Monday the novelty day, a day B reserved for familiar gravy-and-dal plates and where B placed zero of its custom one-offs); and 4 of A's 10 international rows sit on Saturday, where B put 2.

9. The novelty channel runs nearly three times hotter and only in one slot. A introduces 20 exploration dishes in 10 weeks (2.0 per week), every single one a weekday lunch; none at breakfast, on Saturday, or in fruit. B's equivalent, custom one-off dishes, appeared 6 times in 8 weeks (0.75 per week) and roamed: 1 breakfast, 3 weekday lunches, 2 on a Saturday (Dosa and Atta halva). A also never repeats an exploration dish inside the window, while B cooked its one-offs into ordinary weeks.

10. The comfort-repeat rhythm disappears. B repeats a non-staple dish within the same week in 4 of 8 weeks (bread omelette twice in week 2, fish tikka twice in weeks 3 and 4, grilled chicken breast twice in week 7); A does this in 0 of 10 weeks (staples excluded from the test in both: roti, steamed rice, boiled eggs, raita, chutneys). Consecutive weeks also share less: mean Jaccard overlap of the breakfast-plus-lunch dish set between adjacent weeks is 0.114 in A vs 0.173 in B; B's week 2 to 3 transition alone shared at 0.24, higher than any A transition (A's max is 0.16). A reads as a spreader that touches everything once; B reran what it liked while it liked it.

11. Several moderate upward level shifts, consistent in direction. Chicken lunch dishes: A 19 of 60 (0.32 per lunch) vs B 11 of 44 (0.25), about +27 percent per occasion. Dal-named lunch dishes: A 11 of 60 vs B 6 of 44 (+34 percent per occasion); the broader legume family (adding chole, chole bhature, rajma, kadhi, sambar, chana pulao, kosambari) A 20 of 60 vs B 12 of 44 (+22 percent). Plain roti: A 28 in 50 weekday lunches (56 percent, 2.8 per week) vs B 17 in 36 (47 percent, 2.1 per week), roughly +19 percent per occasion. None of these alone is dramatic; together they, plus findings 1, 4, and 5, describe a denser, more protein-and-accompaniment-loaded plate than the household actually ate.

## Too infrequent to call

Mutton is 2 rows in each menu (keema and pepper fry both times). Hummus appears 3 times in A (always a Saturday companion) and 2 in B (both times effectively a weekday main); a real role difference but on 5 total rows. Malai kofta 1 vs 2; kadhi 2 vs 1; butter chicken 1 vs 1; soya chunks masala 1 vs 2; curd rice 1 vs 1. Consecutive-day same-fruit repeats: 2 in A (both papaya, both in Winter) vs 1 in B (mango). Single-dish lunches: 2 in A, 3 in B. B's lone 3-item breakfast (paneer paratha, mint chutney, pav) has no A counterpart. B one-offs absent from A: thepla, pav, anda paratha, dosa, sambar, the custom Red Sauce Pasta breakfast. A one-offs absent from B: poha, sprouts salad, chicken sandwich, paneer sandwich, veg sandwich, paneer bhurji, anda bhurji at breakfast. A repeats a Saturday special twice in 10 weeks (khichdi at a 9-week gap, chole bhature at 10) where B's 8 Saturdays were 8 distinct mains; with only 8 observed weeks in B this is not a callable difference.

## Checked and found the same in both menus

- Boiled eggs: 12 rows in each menu, and the Thursday breakfast lock is a shared trait, 8 of 10 A Thursdays vs 7 of 8 B Thursdays (the role on the plate differs, see finding 3, but the day and the volume match).
- Fish: A 10 of 60 lunches vs B 7 of 44 (0.17 vs 0.16 per lunch); fish tikka is the most frequent named lunch dish in both menus (8 rows in A, 7 in B).
- Prawn: A 5 of 60 vs B 4 of 44 (0.08 vs 0.09 per lunch). Mutton: 2 vs 2.
- Core breakfast rotation, nearly dish for dish: paneer paratha 4 vs 4, vegetable sevai 4 vs 4, bread omelette 5 vs 4, aloo paratha 4 vs 3, besan paneer chilla 3 vs 3, sabudana khichdi 2 vs 2; avocado toast is the top non-egg breakfast in both (10 vs 6).
- Breakfast paneer rate: 9 of 50 (18 percent) vs 7 of 36 (19 percent); the paneer gap of finding 1 is entirely a lunch phenomenon.
- Breakfast chutney rate: 21 of 50 vs 15 of 36, 42 percent in both, drawn from the same four chutneys with garlic then mint on top in both (7 and 6 in A, 6 and 5 in B).
- Specialty rotis: A 7 in 10 weeks vs B 5 in 8 (0.117 vs 0.114 per lunch), beetroot roti ahead of missi roti in both (4 and 3 vs 3 and 2). Steamed rice: 3 rows in each.
- Raita at lunch: A 8 of 60 vs B 5 of 44 (0.13 vs 0.11 per lunch).
- Meal skeleton: no Saturday breakfast in either (0 of 10, 0 of 8); exactly one fruit bowl every present day (60 of 60, 44 of 44); desserts confined to Saturday in both (0 weekday desserts each); every Saturday leads with a one-pot or special main in both.
- Fruit universe and peak: the identical set of 9 fruits appears in both menus (banana, papaya, pomegranate, mango, pineapple, jamun, litchi, plum, peach), and the single most-served fruit holds a 25 percent share in both (pomegranate 15 of 60 in A, mango 11 of 44 in B).
- Lunch-main distinctness: 48 distinct lead dishes in A's 60 lunches (80 percent) vs 34 in B's 44 (77 percent).
- Plate-size extremes: zero 5-item lunches in either menu; 1-item lunches rare in both (2 vs 3).
