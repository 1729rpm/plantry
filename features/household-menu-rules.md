# How this household decides its meals

An observed rulebook, derived from the eight served weeks (2026-06-15 through 2026-08-10) and amended
after an independent review of the rules against those same weeks: the as-eaten slots in prod, every
swap and custom dish, every deletion, and the reasons recorded alongside them. Eight weeks, 44 planned
days, 220 dish placements, 206 hand edits. Roughly half of every week's menu was hand-edited, so the
strongest evidence of intent is what the household swapped in, added, deleted, and said while doing it.
Rules that showed up once or twice are excluded; everything below repeats.

Missing days are normal. Four of a possible 48 days are simply absent: the household drops whole days
without backfilling them, and a plan treats an unplanned day as acceptable, not as a slot that must be
filled.

## Week shape

1. Six days, Monday to Saturday. Breakfast, lunch, and a fruit for the day. No Sunday. Saturday has no
   planned breakfast; it is a lunch-only day.
2. Saturday lunch is the treat meal, and it never repeats: across the 8 observed Saturdays, 8 distinct
   mains (egg biryani, pav bhaji, khichdi with mutton pepper fry, Thai pineapple fried rice with fish
   tikka, chole bhature, baked mozzarella pasta, veg pulao, dosa with sambar). Dishes like pav bhaji,
   chole bhature, dosa, biryani, pasta, and pulao are examples of the register, not a pool to cycle; a
   Saturday main should not reappear within at least two months. The treat element can be the protein
   rather than the carb main: an everyday base (khichdi, pulao) is elevated by a special protein beside
   it (mutton pepper fry, grilled chicken). The plate is the main plus usually one companion (a salad,
   raita, protein, or sabzi) plus a sweet. Sweets appeared on 6 of 8 Saturdays and never on a weekday;
   the sweets themselves rotate widely (5 distinct in 6 servings: sewaiyan kheer twice, kheer,
   shrikhand, fruit custard, atta halva), so no short dessert cycle either. Sweets live on Saturday.

## Meal shape

3. Breakfast is one main plus at most one small item; never a second main. The small item is a chutney
   (15 placements) or boiled eggs riding along as the protein (12 placements beside sevai, upma,
   sabudana khichdi, grilled cheese, masala oats). A chutney appears only beside a paratha, a chilla, or
   standalone boiled eggs (15 of 15 chutney mornings); no other breakfast main takes one. Boiled eggs
   also stand alone as the whole breakfast, with just a chutney (4 times, all in the later weeks); on
   those mornings the eggs are the main, not the side. Egg in some form (boiled eggs, bread omelette,
   anda paratha) opens about half of all mornings (17 of 36).
4. Lunch is two or three items on almost every day (38 of 44): one star dish (a protein, a gravy, or a
   hearty dal or legume), a supporting dal or sabzi, and a carb. A rich or self-contained main
   occasionally stands entirely alone (3 one-item lunches: mutton keema, veg hakka noodles, Thai basil
   chicken). Four items happened only 3 times, and hand-trimming followed; five never happened.
5. One gravy per lunch at most. 23 days had exactly one, 21 had none, no day had two. Stated directly:
   "2 gravy dishes already".
6. Plain roti is the default lunch carb, about twice a week (17 of 44 lunch days), on no fixed day.
   Beetroot roti and missi roti are occasional substitutions for plain roti, together about once every
   week or two (beetroot 3, missi 2, in 8 weeks); at most one specialty roti in a week, and never as a
   weekly fixture on a set day. Rice is the change of pace (about 1 to 2 rice-based lunches a week
   counting steamed rice, curd rice, khichdi, pulao, and biryani), and preferably not on consecutive
   days. Stated directly: "Thursday also have rice. Don't have rice on continuous days".
7. Self-contained mains split in two. Complete plates (biryani, pav bhaji, chole bhature, dosa with
   sambar, khichdi, pulao, and rich single mains like mutton keema) carry the meal alone or with one
   small companion; items placed next to them get deleted ("Not needed with pasta", "Too much food").
   Carb-forward international mains (noodles, fried rice, pasta) are different: they usually take
   exactly one dry protein alongside (6 of 8 placements: chicken tikka, fish tikka twice, grilled
   chicken breast twice, paneer manchurian), always a grilled, tikka, or dry-fry preparation, never a
   gravy, and nothing else on the plate. Deletions outnumber additions 46 to 26 overall; when in doubt
   the household makes the plate smaller, not bigger, but a solo bowl of noodles or pasta is usually one
   item too small.

## Protein

8. Animal protein on most days: 32 of 44 had egg, chicken, fish, prawn, or mutton somewhere, and 36 of
   44 had at least paneer. Lunch carries an animal protein on a little over half of all days.
9. The protein ladder, by days present in 8 weeks: egg (18), chicken (11), paneer (10), fish (7), prawn
   (4), mutton (2). Egg lives mostly at breakfast (2 to 3 mornings a week); chicken, fish, and prawn
   live at lunch. Seafood lands once or twice a week. Chicken averages 1 to 2 lunches a week and never
   more than 2; chicken-free weeks are normal (2 of the 8 weeks had none). When a day needs a protein
   and chicken has had its share, the fill-in order is egg, fish or prawn, paneer, or a dal main;
   chicken is not the default filler. Mutton is a treat-register protein, about twice in 8 weeks,
   usually on or near Saturday.

9a. Paneer is one budget across the whole day and week: besan paneer chilla and paneer paratha at
breakfast count against the same allowance as paneer lunch mains. The allowance is about one paneer
appearance a week on average (10 in 8 weeks: 3 chillas, 4 parathas, 3 lunch mains), ranging from
zero (3 of 8 weeks) to three; two in a week is the comfortable ceiling and paneer is never the
default substitute when another protein is unavailable.

10. Lean grilled or tikka preparations are the everyday protein: fish tikka is the single most repeated
    lunch dish (7 placements in 8 weeks, across five different weekdays), then grilled chicken breast
    and prawn pepper fry. Fish tikka is a weekday lead protein about once a week and also serves as the
    protein partner beside a carb main; it is never merely a garnish on an already-full plate. Rich
    protein gravies (butter chicken, malai kofta, palak chicken) appear about once a week, not daily.
11. Do not repeat a protein across the same day's two meals. Stated directly: palak paneer was swapped
    to palak chicken because "We have paneer in breakfast as well".
12. Tofu is rejected outright, and soya offered as the day's protein is rejected (tandoori soya,
    soyabean curry, thai red curry tofu all swapped away, always toward a real animal protein, paneer,
    or a dal). But soya chunks masala survives as an occasional homely veg main, about twice in 8
    weeks, served like a sabzi with roti; it counts as a veg dish, not as the day's protein, and a day
    that carries it still wants its protein elsewhere.

## Variety and repetition

**Nothing is scheduled by weekday except two anchors.** The week has exactly two day-of-week
structures: Saturday is the treat lunch (rule 2), and Thursday breakfast is egg-anchored, usually a
light grain main (sevai, upma, sabudana khichdi) with boiled eggs (7 of 8 Thursdays). No other dish,
chutney, carb, cuisine, or fruit belongs to a day. A dish that appeared in a given weekday slot last
week should usually appear in a different slot, or not at all, this week; only the favorite set (rule 16) may reoccupy a slot in consecutive weeks, and even favorites drift across days. When a rule below
says "about once a week", that means once somewhere in the week, on a floating day, not once on a
fixed day.

13. The repertoire is compact and familiar but not frozen. Breakfast rotates through about a dozen
    mains: boiled eggs (standalone or as the rider), bread omelette, avocado toast, aloo and paneer
    paratha, besan paneer chilla, vegetable sevai, sabudana khichdi, upma, masala oats, with occasional
    one-offs (thepla, anda paratha, grilled cheese sandwich, a leftover-style pasta). Chutneys rotate
    too (garlic, mint, peanut, coriander) with no chutney tied to a day. The pool itself drifts across
    weeks: heavier parathas cluster in some stretches, lighter eggs-and-chutney or sabudana mornings in
    others, and a new breakfast enters about once a month. Lunch draws on a core of household staples;
    unfamiliar or restaurant-style dishes (rogan josh, ratatouille, korean tofu soup, pesto pasta,
    caprese) get swapped back to staples.
14. A dal-family dish (dal, kadhi, chole, rajma, sambar) appears once or twice a week (10 placements in
    8 weeks, present in 7 of 8 weeks), always alongside roti or rice. About half the time it is the star
    of the meal, not a side: chole with rice and raita, panchmel dal, dal palak, rajma chawal, and
    sambar with dosa all led their plates. A dal-led lunch is a normal veg day, not a fallback.
15. Non-Indian lunches (noodles or fried rice, pasta, Thai, continental, hummus) average about one a
    week, sometimes two, occasionally none, rarely more (observed per-week counts: 0, 2, 2, 2, 1, 4, 1,
    1). Zero-international weeks are normal, not a gap to fill. They land on no fixed day and may take
    the Saturday treat slot (2 of 8 Saturdays: Thai pineapple fried rice, baked mozzarella pasta). The
    household often picks them itself by swap (red sauce pasta, singapore noodles, hummus, veg fried
    rice all swapped in by hand), so they behave like cravings, not like a scheduled category.
16. A favorite main may repeat within the same week, but only from the small favorite set (fish tikka,
    grilled chicken breast, prawn pepper fry, bread omelette, avocado toast, boiled eggs); ordinary
    dishes do not repeat inside a week, and an ordinary main that appeared in the last two weeks usually
    stays off the menu.

16a. **The repetition budget.** Familiar does not mean repetitive. Over a season, roughly three
quarters of distinct lunch mains appear only once or twice (in 8 weeks: 34 distinct mains filled 44
lunch slots, and about 24 of them appeared exactly once). A plan whose top dishes recur 3 to 4 times
each in the same slots is wrong even if every individual dish is a household staple. Ordinary mains
that do recur come back after a gap of two weeks or more (soya chunks masala, malai kofta, palak
chicken, singapore noodles, hummus all returned at 2 to 4 week gaps). One-off dishes each week (curd
rice, thepla, vegetable korma, mushroom matar, stuffed capsicum, continental baked vegetables) are
the normal texture of the menu, not exceptions.

17. A fresh or curd element (salad, raita, curd rice) joins lunch on about a third of days, at most one
    per plate. Stated directly: "Good to have raw vegetables". Raita and salad carry it in roughly
    equal measure (6 salads, 6 raita/curd items in 8 weeks); do not default to salad. Salads run in
    bursts rather than evenly (5 of the 6 fell in the first two weeks), so a stretch with no salads and
    only raita is normal.
18. The daily fruit is chosen, not accepted. Every week draws at least 4 distinct fruits (observed range
    4 to 6 per week), and a full season uses the whole set of about nine (mango, papaya, pomegranate,
    pineapple, jamun, banana, litchi, plum, peach). The in-season favorite leads: during its season
    mango appears 1 to 2 times a week (11 of 44 days) and is the single most served fruit. No fruit
    exceeds twice in any one week, and the same fruit on consecutive days is rare (it happened once).
    Banana, papaya, and pomegranate are the year-round base, but together they fill only about 40% of
    days; they are never a fallback rotation that crowds out the seasonal set. Short-season fruits
    (jamun, litchi, plum, peach) each get a run of 2 to 4 appearances while available, then leave. The
    Winter record is empty (all eight observed weeks fall in Monsoon), so Winter fruit habits are
    unobserved.
19. Named personal anchors recur: avocado toast about once a week by request, rajma because "Tuhina
    loves rajma", and an occasional craving is simply honored ("Craving it").

## The underlying instincts

Four instincts explain most of the 206 hand edits: keep the plate light (trim anything not needed), get
a protein into every day without repeating it within the day, rotate a compact set of familiar staples
rather than chase novelty, and concentrate indulgence (fried, sweet, special) into Saturday. New dishes
do enter, but as homely single additions (stuffed capsicum, cabbage matar aloo, dosa, paneer
manchurian), one or two a month, not as a stream of novelties. A fifth instinct governs the calendar:
apart from the two anchors named at the head of "Variety and repetition" (the Saturday treat lunch and
the Thursday egg breakfast), nothing belongs to a weekday, so every frequency above floats across the
week rather than settling onto a fixed day.
