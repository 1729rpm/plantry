# Reviewer report: Menu A (v6 round-2 proposed 10-week plan) vs Menu B (actual 8 cooked weeks)

Clean-room comparison of the two menus as data. Inputs were only the 10 "Week of" sections of `engine-v6-dry-run-2.md` (Menu A) and `as-eaten-8-weeks.md` (Menu B). The 60-week gate section and the preamble's own measurements were not used as evidence; every number below was counted from the menu text.

## Tabulation

Menu A: 10 weeks (2026-08-31 to 2026-11-02; 5 labeled Monsoon, 5 Winter), 60 days, no skips. 50 breakfasts (Mon to Fri only), 60 lunches, 60 fruit entries. Every Saturday has lunch and fruit, no breakfast.

Menu B: 8 weeks (2026-06-15 to 2026-08-10, with no week of 2026-07-27), 44 days present after 4 skips (W1 Friday; W7 Monday and Tuesday; W8 Monday). 36 breakfasts (8 Saturdays carry none), 44 lunches, 44 fruit entries. Weekday lunches: 36. Because B's skipped days are all weekdays, rates below are normalized to occasions present (per lunch, per breakfast, per Saturday), with per-week figures given as a secondary view.

Slot shape is identical in both: breakfast is a main plus at most one small item (one 3-item breakfast in B, none in A); lunch is 1 to 4 items; fruit is one "X bowl" per day, Saturdays included.

## Findings, most systematic first

1. Saturday is fully standardized in Menu A; in Menu B it was a loose day. A: exactly 3 lunch items on 10 of 10 Saturdays, and a dessert on 10 of 10. B: 3 items on 7 of 8 (one 2-item Saturday), dessert on 6 of 8; B's two dessert-less Saturdays were plain savory plates (Khichdi, Mutton pepper fry, Raita; Fish tikka, Thai pineapple fried rice, Aloo matar) and that shape never occurs in A. A also repeats Saturday mains where B never did: 8 distinct mains across A's 10 Saturdays (Khichdi twice, Singapore noodles twice) versus 8 distinct across B's 8; A's two Khichdi Saturdays (weeks 1 and 10) are the same plate except the dessert (Khichdi, Grilled chicken breast, then Kheer versus Fruit custard).

2. International food migrates from midweek to Saturday. Per occasion the totals are close (A 20 international items over 60 lunches, 33 percent; B 13 over 44, 30 percent), but the placement inverts. Saturdays carrying an international item: A 7 of 10 (8 items; 6 of 10 if Hummus is not counted as international) versus B 2 of 8. Weekday lunches carrying one: A 10 of 50 occasions, 12 items (24 percent of weekday lunches) versus B 11 of 36 (31 percent). B's habit was early-midweek: 9 of its 13 international items fell on Tuesday or Wednesday, with a Tuesday international in 5 consecutive weeks (2026-06-22 through 2026-07-20). A never places a weekday international on Wednesday or Friday (Mon 4, Tue 5, Thu 3 items). Aside: the file's preamble claims an international item on 8 of 10 Saturdays; the menu text shows 7 of 10.

3. Carb-forward international mains lose their protein partner, specifically on Saturday. In B, of 9 lunches led by a vegetarian carb international (pastas, noodles, fried rices, Thai pineapple fried rice, Continental baked vegetables), 7 carried a substantial protein alongside (Grilled chicken breast 3, Fish tikka 2, Chicken tikka 1, custom Paneer Manchurian 1). In A the pairing survives only on weekdays: 4 of 11 such plates get a protein (risotto with grilled chicken, hakka noodles with Thai basil chicken, pasta pomodoro with chicken tikka, red sauce pasta with paneer tikka), while all 6 Saturday carb-international plates take raita, salad, or hummus and zero protein. Related: protein-less lunch plates are 7 of 60 in A and all 7 are Saturdays (7 of 10 Saturdays, counting the mozzarella pasta plate), versus 2 of 8 Saturdays in B; conversely A has zero protein-less weekday lunches in 50 while B has 3 in 36.

4. Novelty is confined to weekday lunch and front-loaded onto Monday and Tuesday. A introduces 20 lunch dishes that never appear in B (2.0 per week), 16 of the 20 exploration placements on Monday or Tuesday; Monday carries an exploration item in 10 of 10 weeks and the Monday main itself is a never-seen dish in 9 of 10. B's 6 Mondays were all familiar staples, and its novelty was 6 custom one-offs in 8 weeks (0.75 per week) scattered across Tuesday, Wednesday, and Saturday, including a breakfast (custom Red Sauce Pasta) and a Saturday main and dessert (Dosa, Atta halva). A adds nothing new anywhere else: its 12 breakfast mains are all within B's 14 (B's Thepla and the custom pasta are dropped), its 9 fruits are exactly B's 9, and its 4 desserts are exactly B's 4 core desserts (the novel Atta halva is dropped).

5. Cadence: Menu A is metronomic where Menu B was bursty. A holds per-week counts nearly constant: plain Roti exactly 2 per week in 9 of 10 weeks, fish exactly 1 per week in 8 of 10, salad exactly 1 per week in 8 of 10; staples return on near-even gaps (Chole in weeks 1, 5, 9; Khichdi 1, 6, 10; Palak chicken gravy 1, 6, 10; Steamed rice 1, 6, 9; Moong dal 2, 5, 8, 10; Prawn pepper fry 2, 5, 7, 10; Hummus 3, 6, 10). B clusters and abandons: Bhindi all 3 servings in weeks 1 to 3 then never again; Raita 4 servings in weeks 3 to 5; 5 of its 6 salads in weeks 1 and 2; weekly fish count swings 0 to 3; Fish tikka lands twice in one week in weeks 3 and 4, then is absent in 3 of the last 4 weeks.

6. Within-week repetition of non-carb items exists only in B. A repeats nothing inside a week except plain Roti (2 or 3 servings, 9 of 10 weeks); 0 of 10 weeks repeat any protein or side. B repeats a non-carb item in 3 of 8 weeks: Fish tikka twice in week 3, Fish tikka and Raita twice each in week 4, Grilled chicken breast twice in week 7. Plain Roti behaves identically in both (weekly sequence A: 1,2,3,2,2,2,2,2,2,2; B: 1,2,3,3,2,2,2,2).

7. Salads and specialty rotis are over-served in A. Salads: 11 of 60 lunches (18.3 percent, 1.10 per week) versus 6 of 44 (13.6 percent, 0.75 per week), a 34 percent per-occasion increase; note B's salads were also front-loaded (5 of 6 in the first two weeks) where A spreads them one per week. Specialty roti: 11 of 60 (18.3 percent; Beetroot 6, Missi 5) versus 5 of 44 (11.4 percent; Beetroot 3, Missi 2), a 61 percent per-occasion increase. The sibling category does not move: raita plus curd rice is 7 in A (0.70 per week) versus 6 in B (0.75 per week).

8. Egg-family lunches jump roughly fivefold. A serves 6 egg-based lunch mains in 60 lunches (Egg pulao, Egg curry, Egg biryani, Japanese egg fried rice, Shakshuka, Spanish omelette; 0.60 per week, and 5 of the 6 are exploration picks) versus 1 in B's 44 (Egg biryani, 0.125 per week). This is nearly the entire animal-protein gap between the menus: lunches carrying any animal protein are 40 of 60 in A (67 percent) versus 25 of 44 in B (57 percent), while chicken (26.7 versus 25 percent of lunches), fish (16.7 versus 15.9), prawn (8.3 versus 9.1), and mutton (5.0 versus 4.5) are each within about 2 points per occasion.

9. Fruit: concentration is flattened, and Winter collapses into a fixed 3-fruit loop. B: 9 distinct fruits with mango dominant at 11 of 44 days (25 percent). A's 5 Monsoon weeks: the same 9 fruits, top fruit mango at only 5 of 30 (17 percent), no fruit more than twice a week, no consecutive-day repeats. A's 5 Winter weeks: every single week is the identical multiset (banana twice, papaya twice, pomegranate twice), 3 distinct fruits total, and 5 consecutive-day repeats versus 1 in all of B. The season windows differ (B is June to August), so the Winter pool itself is partly seasonal; the weekly-identical multiset and the repeat pattern are the menu's own.

10. Weekday habits: one kept, several broken. Kept: boiled eggs at Thursday breakfast (A 10 of 10 Thursdays; B 7 of 8). Broken: Vegetable sevai was a Thursday dish in B (4 of 4 on Thursday) but sits on Thursday only 2 of 5 times in A; Avocado toast was a Tuesday habit in B (4 of 6 on Tuesday) but A rotates it (Mon 3, Fri 3, Tue 2, Wed 2) while regularizing it to exactly once every week (10 of 10 weeks versus 6 of 8 in B; per-breakfast rate is similar, 20 versus 17 percent, and it is standalone every time in both, 10 of 10 and 6 of 6); Bread omelette was a Monday dish in B (3 of 4) but never falls on Monday in A (0 of 5; 3 of 5 on Friday); Prawn pepper fry was Thursday-only in B (3 of 3) and never touches Thursday in A (0 of 5 prawn placements), though n is small.

11. Chutney accompanies breakfast more often in A: 27 of 50 breakfasts (54 percent) versus 15 of 36 (42 percent). The palette is identical, the same four chutneys with garlic and mint leading in both (A: garlic 9, mint 8, peanut 6, coriander 4; B: garlic 6, mint 5, peanut 3, coriander 1).

12. Lunch mains are recycled slightly more in A: 41 distinct first-listed mains over 60 lunches (68 percent) versus 34 over 44 (77 percent). Fish tikka is the workhorse in both, but A serves it as the main 7 times spread over 7 different weeks (never twice a week), while B's 4 main placements include a twice-in-one-week burst. A's longer horizon accounts for part of this gap.

13. Completeness and shape discipline: A plans all 60 days with no skips, no 2-item Saturday, and no breakfast above 2 items; B skipped 4 of 48 days, ran one 2-item Saturday, and once served a 3-item breakfast (Paneer paratha, Mint chutney, Pav). A's exploration also produces plate shapes B never cooked, such as a lone Penne arrabbiata or Spanish omelette as the entire lunch on a Monday (2 occurrences, versus B's 3 one-item lunches which were all familiar dishes).

## Too infrequent to call

Mutton lunches (3 in A vs 2 in B); soya chunks masala (3 vs 2); Hummus's role shift (weekday main twice in B, Saturday side twice plus one weekday side in A); one-item lunches (2 vs 3); 4-item plates' day placement (A's 4 sit mid-to-late week, B's 3 sit Monday and Tuesday); Kadhi (2 vs 1); Malai kofta (2 vs 2); Tomato soup as a lunch companion (1 in A, 0 in B); Khichdi-plus-meat Saturdays (2 vs 1); Grilled cheese sandwich (2 vs 1); Anda paratha (2 vs 1); Sabudana khichdi (3 vs 2); Masala oats (1 vs 1); Curd rice (1 vs 1); Peach (2 vs 2).

## Checked and found the same in both menus

1. Plain Roti: 20 of 60 lunches vs 17 of 44 (2.0 vs 2.1 per week), with near-identical weekly sequences (see finding 6).
2. Dal and legume family: 15 of 60 vs 12 of 44, exactly 1.50 per week in both; dal-led mains 4 in each (0.4 vs 0.5 per week).
3. Fish overall: 10 of 60 (1.0 per week) vs 7 of 44 (0.875 per week); Fish tikka the single most frequent lunch protein in both (8 vs 7 total placements).
4. Chicken per occasion: 16 of 60 (26.7 percent) vs 11 of 44 (25 percent).
5. Prawn: 5 of 60 vs 4 of 44 (0.50 per week in both).
6. Paneer: lunch paneer including Malai kofta 7 of 60 vs 6 of 44 (counting the custom Paneer Manchurian); paneer breakfast mains 9 of 50 vs 7 of 36.
7. Raita plus curd rice: 7 vs 6 (0.70 vs 0.75 per week).
8. Carb presence at weekday lunch (roti, rice, or a rice-based main): 40 of 50 (80 percent) vs 28 of 36 (78 percent); Steamed rice 3 vs 3.
9. Lunch plate size: mode 3 items in both (40 of 60 vs 25 of 44), 4-item share 4 of 60 (6.7 percent) vs 3 of 44 (6.8 percent), mean 2.77 vs 2.64 items.
10. Breakfast shape: main plus at most one small item (A 50 of 50 within 2 items, B 35 of 36); standalone breakfasts 16 of 50 (32 percent) vs 13 of 36 (36 percent), led by Avocado toast and Bread omelette in both.
11. Egg at breakfast: 21 of 50 breakfasts (42 percent) vs 17 of 36 (47 percent); Boiled eggs specifically 14 of 50 (28 percent) vs 12 of 36 (33 percent), Thursday-anchored in both (10 of 10 vs 7 of 8 Thursdays).
12. Saturday scaffolding: no Saturday breakfast in either (0 of 10, 0 of 8); fruit served every day including Saturday (60 of 60, 44 of 44); dessert appears only on Saturday in both (0 non-Saturday desserts each).
13. Fruit vocabulary and format: the identical 9 fruits (banana, papaya, mango, pomegranate, pineapple, jamun, litchi, plum, peach), always one "X bowl" per day, and neither menu ever serves one fruit more than twice in a week (max 2 in both).
14. Dessert palette: A's four (Kheer, Sewaiyan kheer, Shrikhand, Fruit custard) are exactly B's four core desserts.
15. Chutney palette: the same four kinds, same rank order.
16. Breakfast vocabulary: A's 12 mains are a strict subset of B's 14; A introduces no new breakfast.
17. Week-to-week overlap: consecutive-week Jaccard on full item sets 0.189 (A) vs 0.207 (B), close, with A marginally lower.
18. Exact whole-plate repeats: neither menu repeats a byte-identical full lunch plate (0 in both; A's closest is the Khichdi Saturday pair differing only in dessert).
19. Avocado toast per-breakfast rate and form: 10 of 50 (20 percent) vs 6 of 36 (17 percent), standalone every single time in both.
