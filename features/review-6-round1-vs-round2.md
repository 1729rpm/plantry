# Reviewer report: v6 round 1 vs round 2, resolution audit and regression hunt

Basis: round 2 (`features/engine-v6-dry-run-2.md`, 10 weeks, 60 days, 50 breakfasts, 60 lunches) measured on every axis of review-3's 11 findings and review-4 Part 2's regressions, with review-3's counting methods (name-based categories, rates normalized to occasions present), against round 1 (`features/engine-v6-dry-run-menu.md`) and the household's 8 as-eaten weeks (`features/as-eaten-8-weeks.md`, 44 days, 36 breakfasts, 44 lunches). All counts were re-tabulated from the raw menu text of all three files. Numbers in each verdict read (round 1, round 2, household).

**Verdict.** The seven amendments land. Round 2 resolves or improves every one of the 17 audited items and worsens none at the headline level: paneer, chicken, dal, plain roti, and international all sit at or near household rates, plates are household-sized, protein salads are gone, and the protein floor fires 9 times instead of 23. Scoreboard across review-3's findings: 4 resolved, 4 improved, 3 persist; across review-4's six regressions: 3 resolved, 3 improved. What persists is placement and rigidity, not level: the novelty channel still runs at 2.0 picks per week in one slot (now Monday 10 of 10), the household's weekday signatures stay erased, and the Winter fruit pool stays at 3 (content, §14.4). The regression hunt finds five real side effects, the largest being same-family exploration pairs (5 of 10 weeks propose two dishes from one protein family, a family-governor artifact), Saturday flipping to a majority-international day (6 of 10 treats vs the household's 2 of 8), and a breakfast pool that narrowed below household variety when the seed weeks were dropped. The 60-week section then complicates the good 10-week fidelity read: chicken and mutton pass at 10 weeks but fail by week 60 through the self-feed ratchet, while egg converges to almost exact; the 10-week and 60-week windows disagree on nine axes, listed in Part 3.

## Part 2: new-regression hunt

Regressions found (round 2 moved away from the household relative to round 1, or shows a behavior neither round 1 nor the household shows):

1. **Same-family exploration pairs (family governor side effect).** In 5 of 10 weeks both weekly picks come from one protein family: Egg pulao with Egg curry (week 1), Paneer fried rice with Paneer bhurji keto (week 3), Penne arrabbiata with Pasta pomodoro (week 8), Fish curry with Fish fry (week 9), Spanish omelette with Shakshuka (week 10). Round 1 did this twice; the household's one-offs never clustered by ingredient. The governor demotes every at-rate family en bloc, so the top of the surviving pool is the same starved family for both picks; the approximation note admits week 1 can pair, but weeks 8 to 10 pair with the governor fully armed. Week 9 also stacks three fish lunch mains in four days (curry, fry, tikka), a monotone stretch the household never produced.
2. **Saturday becomes the international day.** International treats on 6 of 10 round-2 Saturdays (0.60 per week) vs 1 of 10 treats in round 1 and 2 of 8 household Saturdays (0.25 per week). This inverts the household's habit of internationals on weekdays (5 of 7 Tuesdays) and is the register's own composition surfacing through the treat rotation; the 60-week run confirms it steady at 25 of 41 Saturdays, a gate 4 category fail.
3. **Saturday treat distinctness regresses.** Singapore noodles repeats at a 5-Saturday gap (weeks 2 and 7, a gate 5 fail round 1 passed), and the Khichdi plus Grilled chicken breast plate recurs as a near-identical composition (weeks 1 and 10). Round 1's two repeats sat 9 and 10 weeks apart; the household's 8 Saturdays were 8 distinct mains. The 60-week run shows this is structural (Khichdi 10 of 41, Singapore noodles 8 of 41).
4. **Breakfast chutney inflation.** Chutney on 27 of 50 breakfast days (54 percent) vs 21 of 50 (42 percent) in round 1 and 15 of 36 (42 percent) in the household; round 1 matched the household exactly and round 2 moved away. The seeded cold start hands every chutney a positive backdated deficit, so the positive-deficit small-item rule fires well above the household's appetite. It also produces pairings the record never shows, grilled cheese sandwich with garlic chutney and with mint chutney.
5. **Breakfast variety narrows below the household.** 12 distinct breakfast mains in 50 breakfasts (0.24 per breakfast) vs round 1's 18 (0.36, exactly household-level diversity) and the household's 14 in 36 (0.39). Dropping the seed weeks correctly removed the never-eaten sandwiches, poha, and sprouts salad, but nothing replaces the lost variety because exploration proposes only weekday lunches; the breakfast rotation is now tighter than the household's own.
6. **Consecutive-day fruit repeats rise.** 5 within-week back-to-back repeats (banana and papaya, all in Winter) vs 2 in round 1 and 1 in the household. The all-season fallback plus the 3-fruit Winter pool trades round 1's clean alternation for adjacency; the 60-week run has consecutive repeats in 20 of 41 weeks.

Hunted and clear (amendment side effects looked for and not found):

- **Day-scoped floor leaving protein-less days:** round 2 has 8 days with no meat, egg, or paneer at either meal, exactly the household's 8, and in both menus they are mostly Saturdays; the single round-2 weekday case (2026-10-05 Friday, avocado toast then chole bhature with raita) has direct household analogs. No regression; round 1's near-zero (2) was the anomaly.
- **Optional Saturday accompaniment making Saturdays thin:** every round-2 Saturday holds exactly 3 items, never 2; the option is effectively never exercised, so the risk runs the other way (residual rigidity, item 6 of Part 1), not thinness.
- **Seeded cold start causing an early burst:** week 1 is ordinary in size and repeats nothing; the seeding's visible costs are the chutney inflation (regression 4) and the week-1 egg exploration pair (regression 1), not a volume burst.
- **Dropped seed weeks removing dishes the household wants back:** every dish the household actually ate in the 8 weeks remains reachable, and the losses (sandwich variants, poha, sprouts salad, anda bhurji) never appear in the as-eaten record; the real cost is the aggregate variety loss of regression 5.
- One oddity worth a line: 2026-10-12 Thursday pairs two international mains on one plate (Veg hakka noodles with Thai basil chicken); the household paired an international carb with a tikka or grilled protein, never with a second international.
