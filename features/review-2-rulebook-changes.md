# Review: changes to the household menu rulebook

Produced 2026-08-25 by an independent review agent whose only inputs were the household menu
rulebook (`features/household-menu-rules.md`), the actual 8 cooked weeks, and the menu-comparison
report (`features/review-1-menu-comparison.md`). The agent verified every reviewer claim against
the actual menu itself before proposing changes. Reproduced verbatim below.

---

## 1. Verdict on the rulebook overall

The rulebook is accurate as a description and defective as an instruction set. Nearly every aggregate it states checks out against Menu B: the animal-protein rate (rulebook: a little over half of lunches; B: 25/44 = 57%), the protein ladder ordering, the Saturday treat-plus-sweet shape (6 of 8 Saturdays), Thursday's egg-anchored breakfast (7 of 8), the one-gravy ceiling (no violations in 44 days), the dessert-only-on-Saturday rule (zero weekday sweets), and fruit every day (44/44).

Its failure mode is systematic and single: it states frequencies and lists example pools without ever saying how the household distributes them, so a planner following it compiles "about once a week" into "every Tuesday" and compiles an example list into a strict rotation. Ten of the reviewer's twelve findings are downstream of this one omission. Menu B's deepest property, which the rulebook never states, is that outside two day-of-week anchors (Saturday treat lunch, Thursday egg breakfast) and a small favorite set, almost nothing repeats on a schedule: 44 lunch slots draw on 34 distinct mains (77% unique), about 24 of which appear exactly once, and 36 breakfasts show no repeating day assignment at all.

The secondary failure is scoping: four rules are written one notch too narrow or too broad (boiled eggs cast as a side only, dal cast as support only, self-contained mains cast as solo, all roti variants merged into "roti", soya rejected as a blanket), and each narrow scoping produced exactly the divergence the reviewer ranked.

So: keep the skeleton, add one governing anti-template principle, and re-scope roughly ten rules. Details below.

## 2. Proposed changes

Each change names the reviewer finding(s) it addresses, its type (amendment, addition, or no-change verdict), the full proposed rule text, and the Menu B evidence.

**Change 1. Add a governing anti-template rule.**
Addresses: finding 1 (rigid breakfast day template, the most systematic divergence), and the mechanism behind findings 3, 4, 5, and the overall-repetition finding.
Type: addition (new rule, placed first under "Variety and repetition", and referenced from "The underlying instincts").

Proposed text:

> **Nothing is scheduled by weekday except two anchors.** The week has exactly two day-of-week structures: Saturday is the treat lunch (rule 2), and Thursday breakfast is egg-anchored, usually a light grain main (sevai, upma, sabudana khichdi) with boiled eggs (7 of 8 Thursdays). No other dish, chutney, carb, cuisine, or fruit belongs to a day. A dish that appeared in a given weekday slot last week should usually appear in a different slot, or not at all, this week; only the favorite set (rule 16) may reoccupy a slot in consecutive weeks, and even favorites drift across days. When a rule below says "about once a week", that means once somewhere in the week, on a floating day, not once on a fixed day.

Evidence: across 36 actual breakfasts, no weekday has a locked dish. Avocado toast, the strongest candidate, holds Tuesday only 4 of 7 times and also lands on a Wednesday and a Thursday. Besan chilla falls on Mon, Tue, and Fri, never Wednesday (0/8). Garlic chutney appears on 3 of 7 Fridays but also on Tue, Thu (twice), and a Tuesday again. Monday's modal breakfast (bread omelette) holds only 3 of 6 Mondays. Fish tikka's 7 placements span five different weekdays (Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri, Sat). The plan's 47/50 template adherence has no counterpart in B.

**Change 2. Amend the fruit rule to state breadth, seasonality lead, and a concentration ceiling.**
Addresses: finding 2 (3-fruit collapse; second most systematic).
Type: amendment of rule 18.

Proposed text (replaces rule 18):

> **18.** The daily fruit is chosen, not accepted. Every week draws at least 4 distinct fruits (observed range 4 to 6 per week), and a full season uses the whole set of about nine (mango, papaya, pomegranate, pineapple, jamun, banana, litchi, plum, peach). The in-season favorite leads: during its season mango appears 1 to 2 times a week (11 of 44 days) and is the single most served fruit. No fruit exceeds twice in any one week, and the same fruit on consecutive days is rare (it happened once). Banana, papaya, and pomegranate are the year-round base, but together they fill only about 40% of days; they are never a fallback rotation that crowds out the seasonal set. Short-season fruits (jamun, litchi, plum, peach) each get a run of 2 to 4 appearances while available, then leave.

Evidence: count of B's 44 fruit days: mango 11, pomegranate 7, papaya 6, banana 4, pineapple 4, jamun 4, litchi 3, plum 3, peach 2. Distinct fruits per week: 5, 4, 5, 5, 6, 4, 4, 5. Banana+papaya+pomegranate = 17/44 (39%) versus the plan's 78% and its final 30/30 streak. Jamun's four appearances all fall in weeks 2 to 4 (its season); litchi's three in weeks 3 to 6; that is the run-then-leave shape the rule now names.

**Change 3. Amend the international-lunch rule: fewer, floating, and Saturday-eligible.**
Addresses: finding 3.
Type: amendment of rule 15. This is a mixed case: the rulebook's count ("one or two") is roughly right but the plan sat at the ceiling every single week, and the day-locking came from the missing anti-template principle (change 1). The amendment fixes the count's center of gravity and makes the floating explicit.

Proposed text (replaces rule 15):

> **15.** Non-Indian lunches (noodles or fried rice, pasta, Thai, continental, hummus) average about one a week, sometimes two, occasionally none, rarely more (observed per-week counts: 0, 2, 2, 2, 1, 4, 1, 1). Zero-international weeks are normal, not a gap to fill. They land on no fixed day and may take the Saturday treat slot (2 of 8 Saturdays: Thai pineapple fried rice, baked mozzarella pasta). The household often picks them itself by swap (red sauce pasta, singapore noodles, hummus, veg fried rice all swapped in by hand), so they behave like cravings, not like a scheduled category.

Evidence: roughly 13 international lunches in 8 weeks (about 1.4 to 1.6/week depending on classification of hummus and Indo-Chinese pairings) against the plan's uniform 2.0 every week; weeks 1 and 8 have zero (or one, counting hummus in week 8), week 6 has four. Landing days: Mon, Tue (often), Wed, Sat; never locked.

**Change 4. Amend the Saturday rule: no repeating main, treat can be the protein, richer plate shape, wide dessert rotation.**
Addresses: finding 4, plus a composition pattern neither document states (see section 3).
Type: amendment of rule 2.

Proposed text (replaces rule 2):

> **2.** Saturday lunch is the treat meal, and it never repeats: across the 8 observed Saturdays, 8 distinct mains (egg biryani, pav bhaji, khichdi with mutton pepper fry, Thai pineapple fried rice with fish tikka, chole bhature, baked mozzarella pasta, veg pulao, dosa with sambar). Dishes like pav bhaji, chole bhature, dosa, biryani, pasta, and pulao are examples of the register, not a pool to cycle; a Saturday main should not reappear within at least two months. The treat element can be the protein rather than the carb main: an everyday base (khichdi, pulao) is elevated by a special protein beside it (mutton pepper fry, grilled chicken). The plate is the main plus usually one companion (a salad, raita, protein, or sabzi) plus a sweet. Sweets appeared on 6 of 8 Saturdays and never on a weekday; the sweets themselves rotate widely (5 distinct in 6 servings: sewaiyan kheer twice, kheer, shrikhand, fruit custard, atta halva), so no short dessert cycle either. Sweets live on Saturday.

Evidence: all 8 Saturday mains distinct versus the plan's 4-main strict cycle; 5 distinct desserts versus the plan's 3-item cycle; the khichdi/mutton and pulao/grilled-chicken Saturdays show the treat-as-protein pattern; 6 of 8 Saturdays carry a third element beyond main+sweet.

**Change 5. Amend the carb rule: plain roti is the workhorse, specialty rotis are accents.**
Addresses: finding 5.
Type: amendment of rule 6. The current wording ("Roti is the default carb, on 22 of 44 lunch days, including beetroot and missi variants") is the direct cause: by folding variants into the count it licenses a planner to serve variants at plain roti's frequency.

Proposed text (replaces rule 6):

> **6.** Plain roti is the default lunch carb, about twice a week (17 of 44 lunch days), on no fixed day. Beetroot roti and missi roti are occasional substitutions for plain roti, together about once every week or two (beetroot 3, missi 2, in 8 weeks); at most one specialty roti in a week, and never as a weekly fixture on a set day. Rice is the change of pace (about 1 to 2 rice-based lunches a week counting steamed rice, curd rice, khichdi, pulao, and biryani), and preferably not on consecutive days. Stated directly: "Thursday also have rice. Don't have rice on continuous days".

Evidence: plain roti 17 placements (2.1/week) versus the plan's 1.0/week locked to Monday; beetroot 3 and missi 2 versus the plan's 10 and 10 locked to Thu and Wed. Note one observed exception to the consecutive-rice preference (week of 07-06: khichdi Tuesday, steamed rice Wednesday), which is why the amendment says "preferably", keeping it a soft rule.

**Change 6. Amend the protein ladder with a chicken ceiling and the fill-in order.**
Addresses: finding 6.
Type: amendment of rule 9. The rule states counts but no ceiling, and rule 12's soya ban plus rule 8's protein-most-days push gives a planner chicken as the path of least resistance. The ceiling has to be explicit.

Proposed text (replaces rule 9):

> **9.** The protein ladder, by days present in 8 weeks: egg (18), chicken (11), paneer (10), fish (7), prawn (4), mutton (2). Egg lives mostly at breakfast (2 to 3 mornings a week); chicken, fish, and prawn live at lunch. Seafood lands once or twice a week. Chicken averages 1 to 2 lunches a week and never more than 2; chicken-free weeks are normal (2 of the 8 weeks had none). When a day needs a protein and chicken has had its share, the fill-in order is egg, fish or prawn, paneer, or a dal main; chicken is not the default filler. Mutton is a treat-register protein, about twice in 8 weeks, usually on or near Saturday.

Evidence: chicken 11 lunches in 8 weeks (1.4/week) with zero in the weeks of 07-06 and 08-10, versus the plan's 2.3/week with a minimum of 2 every week; overall non-veg rate is identical in both (0.57 vs 0.60), so the correction is purely a mix shift, which the fill-in order encodes. Mutton: keema (week 1) and pepper fry (week 3 Saturday); the plan served zero.

**Change 7. Amend the self-contained-mains rule: split complete plates from carb-forward mains that take a protein.**
Addresses: finding 7.
Type: amendment of rule 7. The current rule ("carry the meal alone or with one side") actively invites serving noodles and pasta solo; Menu B does the opposite for exactly that subclass.

Proposed text (replaces rule 7):

> **7.** Self-contained mains split in two. Complete plates (biryani, pav bhaji, chole bhature, dosa with sambar, khichdi, pulao, and rich single mains like mutton keema) carry the meal alone or with one small companion; items placed next to them get deleted ("Not needed with pasta", "Too much food"). Carb-forward international mains (noodles, fried rice, pasta) are different: they usually take exactly one dry protein alongside (6 of 8 placements: chicken tikka, fish tikka twice, grilled chicken breast twice, paneer manchurian), always a grilled, tikka, or dry-fry preparation, never a gravy, and nothing else on the plate. Deletions outnumber additions 46 to 26 overall; when in doubt the household makes the plate smaller, not bigger, but a solo bowl of noodles or pasta is usually one item too small.

Evidence: of B's 8 noodle/pasta/fried-rice lunches, 6 carry a protein partner; the only solo servings are veg hakka noodles (a one-item lunch) and baked mozzarella pasta (a Saturday, with dessert). The plan served all 12 of its equivalents solo.

**Change 8. Amend the breakfast-shape rule: boiled eggs can be the main.**
Addresses: finding 8.
Type: amendment of rule 3 (with a matching touch to rule 13's list, folded into change 14). The current wording casts boiled eggs only as "a protein topper", which is why the plan never served them standalone.

Proposed text (replaces rule 3):

> **3.** Breakfast is one main plus at most one small item; never a second main. The small item is a chutney (15 placements) or boiled eggs riding along as the protein (12 placements beside sevai, upma, sabudana khichdi, grilled cheese, masala oats). Boiled eggs also stand alone as the whole breakfast, with just a chutney (4 times, all in the later weeks); on those mornings the eggs are the main, not the side. Egg in some form (boiled eggs, bread omelette, anda paratha) opens about half of all mornings (17 of 36).

Evidence: 12 boiled-egg entries, 4 bread omelettes, 1 anda paratha = 17/36 egg mornings (47%, 2.1/week) versus the plan's 28%; standalone boiled-eggs-with-chutney breakfasts appear 4 times versus 0/50 in the plan.

**Change 9. Add a paneer budget.**
Addresses: finding 9.
Type: addition (new rule under "Protein", after rule 9). The ladder gives paneer's count but nothing stops a planner treating chilla, paratha, and paneer mains as three separate budgets.

Proposed text:

> **9a.** Paneer is one budget across the whole day and week: besan paneer chilla and paneer paratha at breakfast count against the same allowance as paneer lunch mains. The allowance is about one paneer appearance a week on average (10 in 8 weeks: 3 chillas, 4 parathas, 3 lunch mains), ranging from zero (3 of 8 weeks) to three; two in a week is the comfortable ceiling and paneer is never the default substitute when another protein is unavailable.

Evidence: per-week paneer counts in B: 2, 0, 2, 0, 3, 2, 1, 0 (average 1.25/week) versus the plan's 2.4/week with no zero weeks.

**Change 10. Amend the lunch-shape and dal rules: dal can be the star.**
Addresses: finding 10.
Type: amendment of rule 4 and rule 14 together. Rule 4's star definition ("a protein or a gravy") plus rule 14's "always alongside roti or rice" jointly demote dal to a side, which is exactly the plan's error.

Proposed text (replaces rule 4):

> **4.** Lunch is two or three items on almost every day (38 of 44): one star dish (a protein, a gravy, or a hearty dal or legume), a supporting dal or sabzi, and a carb. A rich or self-contained main occasionally stands entirely alone (3 one-item lunches: mutton keema, veg hakka noodles, Thai basil chicken). Four items happened only 3 times, and hand-trimming followed; five never happened.

Proposed text (replaces rule 14):

> **14.** A dal-family dish (dal, kadhi, chole, rajma, sambar) appears once or twice a week (10 placements in 8 weeks, present in 7 of 8 weeks), always alongside roti or rice. About half the time it is the star of the meal, not a side: chole with rice and raita, panchmel dal, dal palak, rajma chawal, and sambar with dosa all led their plates. A dal-led lunch is a normal veg day, not a fallback.

Evidence: dal-family placements: moong dal 3, chole 2, toor dal, kadhi, panchmel dal, rajma chawal, dal palak, sambar. Five of them lead the meal. The plan had 0.7/week, all sides.

**Change 11. Amend the fresh-element rule: raita and salad are equal partners.**
Addresses: finding 11.
Type: amendment of rule 17. The rule's aggregate ("about a third of days") is correct and confirmed (13 of 44 days carry a salad, raita, or curd element, 30%), but the bundling let the plan express it almost entirely as salad.

Proposed text (replaces rule 17):

> **17.** A fresh or curd element (salad, raita, curd rice) joins lunch on about a third of days, at most one per plate. Stated directly: "Good to have raw vegetables". Raita and salad carry it in roughly equal measure (6 salads, 6 raita/curd items in 8 weeks); do not default to salad. Salads run in bursts rather than evenly (5 of the 6 fell in the first two weeks), so a stretch with no salads and only raita is normal.

Evidence: salads: onion tomato (2), mixed veg, cucumber, cucumber tomato, carrot cucumber = 6, five of them in weeks 1 to 2; raita 4 plus cucumber raita 1 plus curd rice 1. The plan ran 1.6 salads/week and 0.3 raitas/week.

**Change 12. Amend the repertoire rule and the fish-tikka rule; add a repetition budget.**
Addresses: finding 12, the overall-repetition finding, and finding 1's dish-level echo.
Type: amendment of rules 10, 13, and 16, plus one addition. Rule 10 is substantively right about fish tikka (case c for the frequency; the plan simply ignored it), but its placement needs one strengthening sentence; rules 13 and 16's "compact and familiar" framing is what a planner over-reads into heavy repetition, so they need the counterweight stated.

Proposed text (replaces rule 10):

> **10.** Lean grilled or tikka preparations are the everyday protein: fish tikka is the single most repeated lunch dish (7 placements in 8 weeks, across five different weekdays), then grilled chicken breast and prawn pepper fry. Fish tikka is a weekday lead protein about once a week and also serves as the protein partner beside a carb main; it is never merely a garnish on an already-full plate. Rich protein gravies (butter chicken, malai kofta, palak chicken) appear about once a week, not daily.

Proposed text (replaces rule 16):

> **16.** A favorite main may repeat within the same week, but only from the small favorite set (fish tikka, grilled chicken breast, prawn pepper fry, bread omelette, avocado toast, boiled eggs); ordinary dishes do not repeat inside a week, and an ordinary main that appeared in the last two weeks usually stays off the menu.

Proposed new rule (addition, under "Variety and repetition"):

> **16a. The repetition budget.** Familiar does not mean repetitive. Over a season, roughly three quarters of distinct lunch mains appear only once or twice (in 8 weeks: 34 distinct mains filled 44 lunch slots, and about 24 of them appeared exactly once). A plan whose top dishes recur 3 to 4 times each in the same slots is wrong even if every individual dish is a household staple. Ordinary mains that do recur come back after a gap of two weeks or more (soya chunks masala, malai kofta, palak chicken, singapore noodles, hummus all returned at 2 to 4 week gaps). One-off dishes each week (curd rice, thepla, vegetable korma, mushroom matar, stuffed capsicum, continental baked vegetables) are the normal texture of the menu, not exceptions.

Evidence: B's 77% unique lunch mains versus the plan's 57%; fish tikka 0.875/week as a weekday lead in B versus 0.5/week in the plan with 4 of 5 placements as a fourth Saturday item; observed comeback gaps all at 2+ weeks.

**Change 13. Amend the soya rule: reject tofu and soya as the protein of the day, keep soya chunks masala as an occasional veg main.**
Addresses: nothing in the reviewer's ranked list (logged under "too infrequent"), but the rulebook as written contradicts Menu B directly, and a planner obeying rule 12 would strip out dishes the household actually kept.
Type: amendment of rule 12.

Proposed text (replaces rule 12):

> **12.** Tofu is rejected outright, and soya offered as the day's protein is rejected (tandoori soya, soyabean curry, thai red curry tofu all swapped away, always toward a real animal protein, paneer, or a dal). But soya chunks masala survives as an occasional homely veg main, about twice in 8 weeks, served like a sabzi with roti; it counts as a veg dish, not as the day's protein, and a day that carries it still wants its protein elsewhere.

Evidence: soya chunks masala appears twice in B as the kept lunch, which the current blanket wording forbids. Both soya-chunk days had eggs at breakfast, supporting the "protein still comes from elsewhere" clause.

**Change 14. Amend the breakfast repertoire list to match the observed pool and its drift.**
Addresses: findings 1 and 8 at the dish level, plus a drift pattern neither document names (section 3).
Type: amendment of rule 13.

Proposed text (replaces rule 13):

> **13.** The repertoire is compact and familiar but not frozen. Breakfast rotates through about a dozen mains: boiled eggs (standalone or as the rider), bread omelette, avocado toast, aloo and paneer paratha, besan paneer chilla, vegetable sevai, sabudana khichdi, upma, masala oats, with occasional one-offs (thepla, anda paratha, grilled cheese sandwich, a leftover-style pasta). Chutneys rotate too (garlic, mint, peanut, coriander) with no chutney tied to a day. The pool itself drifts across weeks: heavier parathas cluster in some stretches, lighter eggs-and-chutney or sabudana mornings in others, and a new breakfast enters about once a month. Lunch draws on a core of household staples; unfamiliar or restaurant-style dishes (rogan josh, ratatouille, korean tofu soup, pesto pasta, caprese) get swapped back to staples.

Evidence: chutney counts: garlic 6, mint 5, peanut 3, coriander 1, spread across all weekdays; new breakfast entrants in sequence: red sauce pasta (wk 4), grilled cheese (wk 5), anda paratha (wk 6), masala oats (wk 8); weeks 6 to 8 shift visibly lighter (4 standalone boiled-egg mornings, 2 sabudana, masala oats, zero aloo parathas) versus parathas concentrated in weeks 1 to 5.

**Change 15. No-change verdicts.**
Addresses: the reviewer's "checked and found the same" list, plus parts of findings 3, 6, and 12.
Type: no change.

The following rules are correct as written and need no amendment, because the plan either matched them or diverged despite them (an execution failure the new rules above fix at the mechanism, not a rulebook defect):

- Rule 1 (six days, Saturday lunch-only): matched exactly in both menus (8/8 and 10/10).
- Rule 2's sweet confinement, retained inside change 4: zero weekday desserts in both menus.
- Rule 5 (one gravy per lunch at most): zero violations in B's 44 days; the plan complied. No change.
- Rule 8 (animal protein on most days; lunch protein a little over half of days): B 25/44 = 0.57 non-veg lunch rate, plan 0.60; matched. No change.
- Rule 11 (no protein repeated across the same day's two meals): zero violations in B; keep verbatim.
- Rule 19 (personal anchors): avocado toast frequency matched (0.75 vs 1.0/week; only the day lock diverged, which change 1 fixes); rajma and craving honoring are intact. No change.
- Prawn and bhindi frequencies: matched (prawn 0.625 vs 0.6/week; bhindi 3 in 8 vs 3 in 10).
- Thursday egg-anchored breakfast: matched (7/8 vs 10/10), and change 1 now names it as one of only two legitimate day anchors.

One caution on the last point: the plan's 10/10 Thursday adherence versus B's 7/8 shows even true anchors are honored at roughly 85 to 90%, not 100%. The anti-template rule's phrasing ("usually") is deliberate.

## 3. Patterns in Menu B that neither the rulebook nor the reviewer covers

1. **The rule-12 contradiction.** Soya chunks masala is a kept dish twice in Menu B while rule 12 declares all soya "consistently rejected". Addressed in change 13. This is the only place the rulebook is factually falsified, rather than under-specified, by its own source data.

2. **Saturday's treat can be the protein, and Saturday plates carry a third element.** Two Saturdays put an everyday base (khichdi, veg pulao) beside a special protein (mutton pepper fry, grilled chicken breast), and 6 of 8 Saturdays have a companion item beyond main+sweet. The rulebook's "one indulgent main plus a sweet" misses both. Addressed in change 4.

3. **One-item lunches exist.** Mutton keema, veg hakka noodles, and Thai basil chicken each stood entirely alone (3 of 44 days). Rule 4's "two or three items on almost every day" is accurate but a planner reading it would never produce a one-item day. Addressed in change 10's amended rule 4.

4. **The rice-consecutive rule has one observed violation** (week of 07-06: khichdi in Tuesday's lunch, steamed rice Wednesday). The rule survives as a preference; change 5 softens it to "preferably".

5. **Breakfast lightness drifts over the season.** Weeks 6 to 8 are measurably lighter (standalone boiled eggs 4 times, sabudana khichdi twice, masala oats; zero aloo parathas after week 5). The rulebook is written as a static distribution; the household's distribution moves. Change 14 names the drift; more generally, any future rulebook revision should weight recent weeks over old ones.

6. **Salad decay is temporal, not just a rate.** 5 of 6 salads fall in the first two weeks (pre-monsoon, plausibly seasonal). A rate-only rule would spread them evenly; change 11 explicitly permits burstiness.

7. **Missing days are normal.** Four of a possible 48 days are simply absent. Neither document notes that the household drops whole days without backfilling them; a plan should treat an unplanned day as acceptable, not as a slot that must be filled. Worth a one-line note in the rulebook's preamble.

8. **A one-off breakfast exception.** One three-item breakfast (paneer paratha, mint chutney, pav) in 36 mornings; correctly excluded under the rulebook's own "rules that showed up once or twice are excluded" standard. No change.

9. **Fruit is always a "bowl"** in B (cut fruit, single variety), never mixed or whole; trivial, but if the plan generates "mixed fruit" entries it would be off-register. No rule proposed; the fruit set in change 2 implies it.
